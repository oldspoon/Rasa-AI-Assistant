Octopi quick start guide

Octopi is a chat assistant for your campus. You type a question, or say it out loud, and it answers like a helpful person at a desk. You do not need any technical knowledge.

Every example below is the real conversation of one student, Alice Johnson. What she types is in the first box, and what Octopi answers her is in the second.

1. Get started: sign in

You sign in once. After that Octopi knows who you are.

  1. Open the Octopi page in your usual web browser.
  2. In the box called Username, type your first name and last name together, with no space and no capital letters. For Alice Johnson that is alicejohnson.
  3. In the box called Password, type your last name and then your first name, again with no space. For Alice Johnson that is johnsonalice.
  4. Press the Sign in button.

If it says your details are wrong, look for a space or a typing slip and try again. As soon as Alice Johnson signs in, Octopi greets her by name:

Great! I found your record, Alice Johnson. You can now ask me about your schedule. For example: 'What days do I have lectures?', 'What days do I have practicals?', 'Give me all my schedule details', or 'When can I visit my teacher?'

2. How to chat

There are four ways to talk to Octopi. Use whichever feels easiest.

  • Type. Click the box at the bottom marked Message, type your question, then press Enter or the Send button. Write the way you would text a friend. You do not need exact words.
  • Tap a button. Octopi often shows buttons under its answer, such as "Borrow book 2" or "Pay by phone". Tapping one is the same as typing it, and it is the easiest way to carry on.
  • Speak. If you see a small microphone, tap it, say your question in English, tap it again to stop, check the words, then press Send.
  • Suggestions. Octopi shows at most three suggested questions or buttons at a time, and it never offers one you have already used. As you use them, new ones appear.
  • Use the shortcuts. The list on the left, under Ask about, sends a common question for you with one tap.

Chat History. Under Conversation on the left, press Chat History to see your earlier conversations on this device. Each one is named after the first thing you asked or the first button you pressed. Choose one to carry on where you left off. History is cleared when you sign off.

Want to start again? Choose New conversation. Finished? Press Sign off at the top, especially on a shared computer.

Borrow a book

Tap a Borrow button, or type the number of the book you want.

I want to borrow book 2

Alice, you picked Lives of the Artists by Giorgio Vasari. How would you like to borrow it? A digital copy is ready straight away. An in-person copy gets you a collection code and a receipt. Buttons: Borrow digitally, Collect in person

Octopi then asks how you would like to borrow it:

  • Borrow digitally. The book is yours straight away in your digital library. It returns by itself after 28 days, so there is nothing to carry back and no fine.
  • Collect in person. You get a collection code and a receipt in the chat. The receipt says where to collect the book, the date to collect it by, the date to bring it back and where to return it. Show the code at the library desk to get your book. Take a screenshot or write the code down.

Everyday chat

Octopi is polite and understands the basics.

Hello

Hello! I'm Octopi, your Campus Attendance Assistant. Welcome to the Campus Attendance System. Ask me about your schedule - lecture or practical days, campus, time, subject, room, your teacher or the library. I can also show your teacher's contact details and office hours, book a visit, lend you books and book extra support lectures. If I don't know you yet, tell me your first and last name (e.g. 'My name is Alice Johnson').

Thank you

You're welcome! Let me know if you need anything else about your schedule.

Goodbye

Goodbye! Have a great session on campus!

Help

I'm Octopi, the Campus Attendance Assistant. Here is what I can do, in plain words:

1. Tell me who you are. Type your first and last name, for example 'My name is Alice Johnson'.
2. Your timetable. Ask about your lecture or practical days, time, room, campus or subject, or say 'Give me all my schedule details'.
3. Your teacher. Ask 'Who is my teacher?', 'How can I contact my teacher?' or 'When can I visit my teacher?', then book a visit, for example 'Book Tuesday at 14:30'.
4. The library. Ask 'Where is the library?', then 'Recommend books' and borrow one digitally or collect it in person.
5. Appointments and timetable. Ask 'Show my appointments' for your booked teacher visits and support lectures, or 'Show my timetable' for your whole week.
6. Extra support. Ask 'What support lectures are available?' to see sessions run by lab assistants, then book a seat.
7. What you owe. Ask 'Do I have any parking arrears?' or 'Show my borrowed books' to check fines.

Just type your question like a text message, tap the buttons I show, or use the microphone to speak.

If something goes wrong

  • Octopi says it did not understand. Phrase it another way, or type "Help".
  • Octopi asks you to introduce yourself. Type "My name is" followed by your first and last name.
  • A banner says the server is asleep. The free hosting takes a short while to wake up. Octopi still answers in the meantime. Try again in a minute.
  • Hard to read the screen? Open Options at the top and switch between the light and dark theme.
  • Something else. Octopi only talks about your timetable, your teacher, the library and your fines. For anything else it will say so politely.
